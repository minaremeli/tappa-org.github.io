The Interview

The Shape of Uncertainty

Philipp Hennig has spent a career arguing that computation itself is a form of inference. Over coffee near the institute, we asked him what that means for the next generation of researchers in Tübingen — and what he wishes someone had told him at the start.

Interview by Haiwen · Photographs by the TAPPA design team ·

There is a particular kind of calm in the way Philipp Hennig talks about hard problems, as though every open question were simply a distribution waiting to be narrowed. We began, as good interviews should, with something small: the walk to work.

“I think better when I'm moving,” he says. “Most of what I believe about probabilistic numerics started as something half-formed on the way between two buildings.” It is a fitting origin for a research programme that treats the act of computing as the act of learning — each step a measurement, each measurement an update.

His point, made gently but insistently, is that uncertainty is not a nuisance to be engineered away. It is information. A model that reports its own confidence honestly is more useful, and more scientific, than one that simply asserts.

“A method that knows what it doesn't know is worth more than one that is merely confident.”

We turned to Tübingen itself. What makes the place work, he suggests, is density without hierarchy: a PhD student can corner a professor at a Friday talk and be taken seriously. “The corridor conversations are the research,” he says. “The papers come later.”

Asked what he would tell a new doctoral student, he pauses longer than for any other question. “Pick a problem you would be happy to fail at for three years. The failing is most of it. If the failing would still be interesting, you've chosen well.”

By the end, the coffee long cold, his optimism had become contagious. The machines, he reminds us, are not the point. “We are building instruments for thinking. The interesting part is what we choose to point them at.”

Life · Ice Cream

Ice Cream Parlours of Tübingen, Rated

Summer came and went and came back again, and there will be more of it — probably even this year, thanks to global heating. All the more important, then, to know where to cool off. A small interdisciplinary, international expert team tested more or less every ice cream parlour in and around Tübingen to produce this obviously exhaustive pro-and-contra list.

By Fynn · The parlours are i.i.d. randomly ordered
